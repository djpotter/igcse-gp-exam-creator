# Source Types Specification

Detailed specifications for creating authentic IGCSE Global Perspectives source materials.

---

## Source Overview

| Source | Type | Word Count | Purpose | Questions |
|--------|------|------------|---------|-----------|
| **1** | Factual Data | 15-80 | Statistics and facts | Q1(a) |
| **2** | Organisation Perspective | 57-131 | Viewpoint with bias | Q1(b), Q1(c), Q1(d) |
| **3** | Student Research | 113-177 | Research methodology | Q2(a), Q2(b) |
| **4** | Debate | 232-269 | Two opposing speakers | Q3 |

## Source 1: Factual Data

### Purpose
Provides objective statistics and facts that students can identify in Q1(a).

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 15-80 words |
| **F-K Grade** | 4.4-10.5 (higher complexity acceptable) |
| **Flesch RE** | 45-90 |
| **Tone** | Neutral, informational |
| **Attribution** | Authoritative source (UN, WHO, government, research body) |

### Content Requirements
- [ ] Contains specific statistics (numbers, percentages, dates)
- [ ] At least ONE clear fact that can be the answer to Q1(a)
- [ ] Source attribution included (organisation name and year)
- [ ] Neutral presentation without opinion or bias

### Format Options
1. **Prose paragraph** - Short informational text
2. **Bullet points** - List of key facts
3. **Table** - Data in tabular format
4. **Definition box** - Key term definition with supporting data

### Example Structures

**Prose Format:**
```
According to the United Nations World Food Programme (2024), approximately
735 million people worldwide face chronic hunger. This represents nearly
10% of the global population. Sub-Saharan Africa has the highest rates
of food insecurity, with 23% of the population affected.
```

**Table Format:**
```
Global Hunger Statistics (UN World Food Programme, 2024)

Region                  | People Facing Hunger | % of Population
------------------------|---------------------|----------------
Sub-Saharan Africa      | 282 million         | 23%
South Asia              | 243 million         | 14%
East Asia               | 89 million          | 4%
Latin America           | 56 million          | 9%
```

**Definition Format:**
```
Food insecurity: When people do not have reliable access to sufficient,
affordable, and nutritious food (World Health Organisation, 2024).

Currently, 2.4 billion people experience moderate or severe food insecurity
globally—approximately 30% of the world's population.
```

## Source 2: Organisation Perspective

### Purpose
Presents a viewpoint from a charity, NGO, or advocacy group. Contains the critical thinking concept for Q1(b) and the perspective for Q1(c).

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 57-131 words |
| **F-K Grade** | 5.2-9.8 |
| **Flesch RE** | 47-71 |
| **Tone** | Persuasive, emotive |
| **Attribution** | Named organisation (charity, NGO, campaign group) |

### Content Requirements
- [ ] Organisation name and type clearly stated
- [ ] **2-3 quotable examples** of the target critical thinking concept
- [ ] ICEVA elements present (Issue, Causes, Effects, Values, Actions)
- [ ] Persuasive/emotive language
- [ ] Clear perspective on the topic

### Critical Thinking Concept Markers

**For GENERALISATION:**
Include words like: "always," "never," "all," "every," "most people," "millions," "everyone"
> "Every day, millions of families struggle to put food on the table."

**For PREDICTION:**
Include future tense markers: "will," "going to," "in the future," "eventually," "by 2050"
> "Without immediate action, hunger will affect over 1 billion people by 2030."

**For CLAIM:**
Include statements presented as fact that require evidence:
> "Local food production is more effective than international aid."

**For OPINION:**
Include personal judgement markers: "we believe," "in our view," "I think," "it seems"
> "We believe that education is the most powerful tool against poverty."

**For VALUE:**
Include importance markers: "important," "should," "must," "right," "wrong," "matters"
> "Every child deserves access to nutritious food—this is a fundamental right."

### Example Structure

```
[Organisation Name] is a [type: charity/NGO/campaign group] dedicated to
[mission statement].

[Statement about the issue - include critical thinking concept marker]

[Cause identification]. [Effect/consequence of the issue].

[Second cause or value statement]. [Proposed action or solution].

[Concluding statement with emotive language and call to action]
```

### Example (Generalisation concept):

```
Feed the Future Alliance is an international charity working to end
global hunger.

Every day, millions of families go to bed hungry while wealthy nations
waste enough food to feed them all. The causes are clear: unfair trade
policies, climate change, and lack of investment in local farming.

Hunger doesn't just cause suffering—it destroys communities and forces
people to migrate in search of food. We believe governments must act now
to support sustainable agriculture and fair food distribution. Without
urgent action, this crisis will only deepen.
```

**Quotable generalisations in this example:**
1. "Every day, millions of families go to bed hungry"
2. "wealthy nations waste enough food to feed them all"
3. "this crisis will only deepen" (also a prediction)

## Source 3: Student Research Project

### Purpose
Describes a fictional student's research project with methodology for Q2(a) evaluation and a testable claim for Q2(b).

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 113-177 words |
| **F-K Grade** | 5.1-9.1 (**EASIER** - student voice) |
| **Flesch RE** | 56-81 |
| **Tone** | First-person, authentic student voice |
| **Voice** | Personal narrative style |

### Content Requirements
- [ ] Student name (first name only)
- [ ] Research location (specific place)
- [ ] Clear methodology description
- [ ] Sample size and selection method
- [ ] Findings with percentages/statistics
- [ ] Built-in **strengths** for evaluation
- [ ] Built-in **weaknesses** for evaluation
- [ ] **Testable claim** in conclusion (for Q2(b))

### Built-in Strengths (include at least 2)
- Reasonable sample size for context
- Clear methodology
- Relevant research questions
- Specific location/context
- Mix of methods (triangulation)
- Appropriate target group

### Built-in Weaknesses (include at least 2)
- Limited sample size
- Convenience sampling
- Single location
- Short time frame
- Self-reported data
- Potential researcher bias
- Leading questions
- No control group
- Limited demographic range

### Example Structure

```
[Student name] carried out research about [topic] in [location].

[Methodology: What they did, sample size, who they surveyed/interviewed]

[Finding 1 with percentage/number]. [Additional detail].

[Finding 2 with percentage/number]. [Additional detail].

[Optional Finding 3].

[Student's conclusion - THIS IS THE TESTABLE CLAIM FOR Q2(b)]

[Built-in limitation acknowledgement - optional but helpful]
```

### Example:

```
Priya carried out research about attitudes to food waste in her school
in Mumbai, India.

She created an online survey and shared it with students in Years 10 and
11 at her school. 87 students completed the survey over two weeks.

Priya found that 72% of students said they throw away food at least once
a week. When asked why, 45% said portions were too large and 38% said
they didn't like the taste.

Interestingly, 81% of students said they felt guilty about wasting food,
but only 23% had ever tried to reduce their food waste.

Priya concluded that most young people feel guilty about food waste but
don't change their behaviour.

She acknowledged that her sample only included students from one school,
which might not represent all young people.
```

**Testable claim:** "Most young people feel guilty about food waste but don't change their behaviour."

**Built-in strengths:** Reasonable sample size (87), clear methodology, specific percentages, relevant topic

**Built-in weaknesses:** Single school, convenience sample, self-reported data, short timeframe

## Source 4: Debate Between Two Speakers

### Purpose
Presents two named individuals with opposing views for argument evaluation in Q3.

### Specifications

| Attribute | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Word Count** | 232-269 words (TOTAL for both speakers) |
| **F-K Grade** | 6.2-9.5 |
| **Flesch RE** | 49-73 |
| **Format** | Two clearly labelled speakers |
| **Balance** | Equal word counts, evidence, and reasoning quality |

### Content Requirements
- [ ] Two named speakers with distinct names
- [ ] Opposing positions clearly stated
- [ ] Each speaker uses reasoning (logical arguments)
- [ ] Each speaker uses evidence (facts, statistics, examples)
- [ ] Each speaker uses emotive/persuasive language
- [ ] Neither argument is obviously "correct"
- [ ] Word counts within 20% of each other

### Balance Checklist
- [ ] Speaker word counts: within 20% of each other
- [ ] Evidence points: roughly equal number
- [ ] Reasoning quality: similarly strong/weak
- [ ] Emotive language: both use some
- [ ] Neither position is a "straw man"

### Speaker Name Guidelines
- Use culturally diverse names
- Avoid stereotypical associations
- Names should be pronounceable internationally
- Don't always give "progressive" views to certain genders/cultures

**Good name pairs:**
- Grace and Seth
- Aput and Panuk
- Fleur and Dion
- Mei and James
- Amara and Viktor

### Example Structure

```
[Speaker A name] and [Speaker B name] are discussing [topic].

[Speaker A]:
[Opening position statement]

[Argument 1 with evidence]. [Development/explanation].

[Argument 2 with reasoning]. [Emotive language element].

[Concluding statement reinforcing position]

[Speaker B]:
[Opening position statement - opposing Speaker A]

[Counter-argument or alternative view with evidence]. [Development].

[Second argument with reasoning]. [Emotive language element].

[Concluding statement reinforcing opposing position]
```

### Example:

```
Grace and Seth are discussing whether schools should teach students
how to grow their own food.

Grace:
Schools absolutely should teach food growing. With climate change
threatening food supplies, the next generation needs practical skills
to feed themselves. Research shows that students who grow food develop
better understanding of nutrition and make healthier choices.

Growing food also teaches patience, responsibility, and respect for
nature—values our screen-obsessed young people desperately need. Every
child deserves to know where their food comes from.

Seth:
While food growing sounds nice, schools have limited time and must
prioritise core subjects. Students need strong literacy, numeracy, and
digital skills to succeed in the modern economy—not gardening.

Food production is increasingly high-tech, using AI and robotics.
Teaching children to dig in soil is preparing them for the past, not
the future. Schools should focus on the skills that will actually help
young people get good jobs and contribute to society.
```

**Balance check:**
- Grace: ~95 words, 2 evidence points, emotive language ("desperately need," "deserves")
- Seth: ~90 words, 2 evidence points, emotive language ("nice," "actually help")
- Neither is obviously "right"

## Source Attribution Guidelines

### Source 1 Attributions
Use authoritative, real or realistic organisations:
- United Nations (UN)
- World Health Organisation (WHO)
- World Food Programme (WFP)
- [Country] Government / Ministry of [X]
- [University] Research Centre
- National Statistics Office

### Source 2 Attributions
Use realistic charity/NGO names:
- [Topic] Alliance
- Global [Topic] Foundation
- [Action] for [Cause]
- International [Topic] Network
- [Place] [Topic] Campaign

**Examples:**
- Feed the Future Alliance
- Global Education Foundation
- Action for Clean Water
- International Migration Network
- Youth Climate Campaign

### Source 3 Attributions
Student first names only:
- Use common international names
- Vary gender and cultural background
- Include school/location context

### Source 4 Attributions
Two first names with brief context:
- "[Name] and [Name] are discussing [topic]"
- "[Name] and [Name] are debating [topic]"
