# Student Feedback Guide

Comprehensive guidance for providing feedback on IGCSE Global Perspectives exam responses.

---

## Feedback Framework

When reviewing student work, assess five key areas:

| Area | What to Look For |
|------|------------------|
| **1. Source Use** | Are they citing specific evidence? |
| **2. ICEVA Coverage** | For Q1(c): Which elements are addressed? |
| **3. Evaluation Depth** | Are strengths AND weaknesses discussed? |
| **4. Comparative Reasoning** | Are alternatives compared? |
| **5. Justified Conclusions** | Is there a clear, reasoned ending? |

---

## Question-Specific Feedback

### Q1(a) - Factual Retrieval [1 mark]

**What to check:**
- Is the answer from Source 1?
- Does it directly answer the question asked?
- Is it the specific fact/figure requested?

**Common errors:**
- Giving information from the wrong source
- Providing related but incorrect information
- Not reading the question carefully (e.g., giving a number when percentage was asked)

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Correct - you identified [fact] from Source 1."
- ✗ "Check the question - it asked for [X] but you gave [Y]."
- ✗ "This information isn't from Source 1."

---

### Q1(b)(i) - Identify Critical Thinking Concept [1 mark]

**What to check:**
- Is the example from Source 2?
- Is it actually an example of the specified concept?
- Is it clearly quoted or identified?

**Common errors by concept:**

| Concept | Common Confusion |
|---------|------------------|
| **Generalisation** | Confusing with fact (generalisations lack evidence for broad claims) |
| **Prediction** | Confusing with claim (predictions are specifically about future) |
| **Claim** | Confusing with fact (claims need verification) |
| **Opinion** | Confusing with value (opinions are personal judgements) |
| **Value** | Confusing with opinion (values are about what's important/right) |

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Good identification - '[quote]' is a [concept] because..."
- ✗ "This is actually a [different concept] because it [explanation]."
- ✗ "This isn't from Source 2 - look there for examples."

---

### Q1(b)(ii) - Explain Why [2 marks]

**What to check:**
- Do they define what the concept means?
- Do they apply the definition to their chosen example?
- Do they identify specific language features?

**Mark allocation:**
- 1 mark: Partial definition OR feature identification
- 2 marks: Clear definition + application to example

**Common errors:**
- Restating the example without explaining why it's that concept
- Vague explanations ("It just is a generalisation")
- Not defining the concept

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Excellent - you explained that a [concept] is [definition] and showed how your example fits."
- △ "You identified it correctly but need to explain WHY it's a [concept]."
- ✗ "Remember to define what a [concept] is, then show how your example matches."

---

### Q1(c) - Describe Perspective [6 marks]

**What to check (ICEVA):**
- [ ] **I**ssue - What problem does the organisation focus on?
- [ ] **C**auses - What reasons do they give for the problem?
- [ ] **E**ffects - What consequences do they highlight?
- [ ] **V**alues - What do they believe is important?
- [ ] **A**ctions - What solutions do they propose?

**Level indicators:**

| Level | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| **L3 (5-6)** | Detailed coverage of multiple ICEVA elements, well-developed points, clear links |
| **L2 (3-4)** | Some ICEVA elements, shows understanding but lacks detail |
| **L1 (1-2)** | Limited/superficial, may be list-like, partially relevant |

**Common errors:**
- Only describing what the source says (not the perspective)
- Missing key ICEVA elements
- List-like responses without development
- Adding own opinion instead of describing the organisation's view

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "You covered the issue, causes, and actions well. Consider also discussing their values."
- △ "Good start identifying the issue, but develop your points about effects and actions."
- ✗ "Remember to describe THEIR perspective, not your own view on the topic."

---

### Q1(d) - Most Significant [8 marks]

**What to check:**
- Do they clearly state which factor is most significant?
- Do they explain WHY it's more significant than others?
- Do they use evidence from sources?
- Do they compare with other factors?
- Do they reach a clear conclusion?

**Level indicators:**

| Level | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| **L4 (7-8)** | Clear choice, thorough justification, comparison with others, strong evidence use |
| **L3 (5-6)** | Clear choice, good justification, some comparison, uses evidence |
| **L2 (3-4)** | Identifies factor, limited justification, little comparison |
| **L1 (1-2)** | Identifies factor with minimal/no explanation |

**Common errors:**
- Describing multiple factors without choosing most significant
- Choosing but not justifying why it's MORE significant than others
- Not using evidence from sources
- No clear conclusion

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Strong argument for why [factor] is most significant, with good comparison to alternatives."
- △ "You explained [factor] well but need to compare - why is it MORE significant than [other factor]?"
- ✗ "Remember to choose ONE factor as MOST significant and justify your choice."

### Q2(a) - Research Evaluation [8 marks]

**What to check:**
- Do they discuss BOTH strengths AND weaknesses?
- Are points specific to THIS research (not generic)?
- Do they explain WHY something is a strength/weakness?
- Do they consider methodology, sample, questions, etc.?

**Evaluation criteria:**
- Sample size and selection
- Research method appropriateness
- Question design (open/closed, leading/neutral)
- Response rate
- Geographic/demographic limitations
- Researcher influence
- Generalisability

**Level indicators:**

| Level | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| **L4 (7-8)** | Thorough evaluation of BOTH, well-developed, sophisticated understanding |
| **L3 (5-6)** | Good evaluation of both, may lack balance or full development |
| **L2 (3-4)** | Some evaluation, may focus on one aspect, underdeveloped |
| **L1 (1-2)** | Limited, may only address strengths OR weaknesses |

**Common errors:**
- Only discussing strengths OR weaknesses (not both)
- Generic points not specific to this research
- Identifying without explaining (e.g., "Small sample" without why that matters)
- Not using information from Source 3

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Excellent balance of strengths and weaknesses with clear explanations of why each matters."
- △ "Good strengths but your weaknesses need more development - explain WHY [point] is a limitation."
- ✗ "You must discuss BOTH strengths AND weaknesses to reach higher levels."

---

### Q2(b) - Test a Claim [8 marks]

**What to check:**
- Do they design research that would actually test the claim?
- Do they include: sample, method, specific questions/procedures?
- Do they explain how findings would test the claim?
- Is the methodology appropriate?

**Key elements:**
- Research question linked to claim
- Target population/sample
- Sample selection method
- Sample size
- Research method (survey, interview, observation)
- Specific questions or procedures
- How data would be analysed
- How findings would test the claim

**Level indicators:**

| Level | Characteristics |
|-------|-----------------|
| **L4 (7-8)** | Clear methodology that directly tests claim, detailed, sophisticated |
| **L3 (5-6)** | Clear methodology, appropriate but may lack detail |
| **L2 (3-4)** | Some methodology, vague or not fully appropriate |
| **L1 (1-2)** | Limited, mentions research without clear methodology |

**Common errors:**
- Describing research that doesn't actually test the specific claim
- Vague methodology ("I would do a survey")
- Missing key elements (no sample size, no specific questions)
- Not explaining how findings would confirm/refute the claim

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Clear methodology that would directly test '[claim]' with specific details."
- △ "Good method choice but add specifics - what questions would you ask? How many people?"
- ✗ "Your research wouldn't test this specific claim - think about what evidence would prove or disprove '[claim]'."

### Q3 - Argument Evaluation [16 marks]

**What to check:**
- Do they analyse BOTH speakers?
- Do they identify reasoning, evidence, AND language?
- Do they evaluate (not just describe)?
- Do they make a clear judgement about which is more convincing?
- Do they support judgement with speakers' words?

**Analysis elements (for each speaker):**
- Main position/argument
- Reasoning (logical arguments)
- Evidence (facts, statistics, examples)
- Language (emotive, persuasive techniques)

**Evaluation elements:**
- Comparison between speakers
- Assessment of argument quality
- Justified judgement
- Use of quotations/specific references

**Level indicators (Analysis + Evaluation):**

| Level | Analysis | Evaluation |
|-------|----------|------------|
| **L4 (7-8)** | Thorough analysis of BOTH, detailed | Clear judgement, well-justified, uses quotes |
| **L3 (5-6)** | Good analysis of both, may lack balance | Judgement with justification, some comparison |
| **L2 (3-4)** | Some analysis, may focus on one speaker | Judgement asserted, limited comparison |
| **L1 (1-2)** | Limited, may describe not analyse | Little/no justified judgement |

**Common errors:**
- Only analysing one speaker
- Describing arguments without evaluating them
- Not making a clear judgement
- Judgement without justification
- Not using speakers' words as evidence

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Strong analysis of both speakers with clear evaluation of why [Speaker] is more convincing."
- △ "Good analysis of [Speaker A] but you need equal coverage of [Speaker B]."
- ✗ "Remember to EVALUATE, not just describe - which argument is stronger and WHY?"

---

### Q4 - Recommendation [20 marks]

**What to check:**
- Do they state a clear recommendation?
- Do they give reasons and evidence?
- Do they use sources AND/OR own ideas?
- Do they consider different perspectives?
- Do they acknowledge alternative actions?

**Reasoning elements:**
- Clear recommendation stated
- Reasons why this action is best
- Evidence from sources
- Own ideas/knowledge
- Explanation of how it addresses the issue

**Evaluation elements:**
- Different perspectives considered
- Alternative actions acknowledged
- Strengths of other options recognised
- Limitations of chosen option considered
- Balanced assessment

**Level indicators (Reasoning + Evaluation):**

| Level | Reasoning | Evaluation |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **L5 (9-10)** | Excellent, well-developed arguments, effective evidence use | Multiple perspectives, thorough consideration of alternatives |
| **L4 (7-8)** | Good arguments, good evidence | Good consideration of perspectives, may lack full balance |
| **L3 (5-6)** | Sound reasoning, some development | Some consideration of alternatives |
| **L2 (3-4)** | Basic reasoning, limited justification | Limited consideration of perspectives |
| **L1 (1-2)** | Limited, preference without justification | Little/no consideration of alternatives |

**Common errors:**
- Not stating clear recommendation
- Only discussing chosen action (not considering alternatives)
- Not using source material
- One-sided argument without different perspectives
- No acknowledgement of limitations

**Feedback stems:**
- ✓ "Strong recommendation with excellent consideration of alternative perspectives."
- △ "Good reasons for your choice, but consider why someone might prefer [other action]."
- ✗ "Remember to consider different arguments and perspectives, not just why your choice is good."

## Common Examiner Insights

Based on examiner reports, students frequently:

1. **Confuse concepts:**
   - Claim vs Fact (claims need verification)
   - Opinion vs Value (values are about importance/rightness)
   - Generalisation vs Fact (generalisations make broad claims)

2. **Describe instead of evaluate:**
   - Q2(a): List features without assessing strengths/weaknesses
   - Q3: Summarise arguments without judging which is better
   - Q4: Describe actions without recommending

3. **Miss the "most significant" element:**
   - Discuss multiple factors equally
   - Don't compare or justify choice

4. **Fail to reach conclusions:**
   - Higher-mark questions need clear conclusions
   - Judgements must be justified, not just stated

5. **Don't evaluate BOTH sides:**
   - Q3: Only analyse one speaker
   - Q4: Only discuss chosen action

6. **Ignore source material:**
   - Don't quote or reference sources
   - Miss opportunities to use evidence

### Development-focused:
"Good attempt at [skill]. You [partial achievement]. To reach higher levels, [specific improvement needed]."

### Foundational:
"You've started to [skill]. Focus on [fundamental requirement] first, then [next step]."
