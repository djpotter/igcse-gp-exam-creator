## Question 1(a) [1 mark]

### Format
Point-based marking. Award 1 mark for correct identification from Source 1.

### Template
```
1(a) [1 mark]
Award 1 mark for correct identification of [specific fact/figure].

Acceptable answers:
• [Answer 1]
• [Answer 2 - if alternative wording acceptable]

Do not accept:
• [Common incorrect answer]
• Answers not from Source 1
```

### Example
```
1(a) [1 mark]
Award 1 mark for correct identification of the percentage of the global
population facing hunger.

Acceptable answers:
• 10%
• Nearly 10%
• About 10 percent
• One in ten

Do not accept:
• 735 million (this is the number, not the percentage asked for)
• Answers that don't match the question
```

## Question 1(b)(i) [1 mark]

### Format
Point-based marking. Award 1 mark for identification of a valid example of the specified critical thinking concept.

### Template
```
1(b)(i) [1 mark]
Award 1 mark for identification of a [concept] from Source 2.

Indicative content - [concepts] include:
• "[Direct quote 1 from Source 2]"
• "[Direct quote 2 from Source 2]"
• "[Direct quote 3 from Source 2]"
• Any other valid [concept] from Source 2

Do not accept:
• Examples not from Source 2
• Statements that are not [concepts]
```

### Example (Generalisation)
```
1(b)(i) [1 mark]
Award 1 mark for identification of a generalisation from Source 2.

Indicative content - generalisations include:
• "Every day, millions of families go to bed hungry"
• "wealthy nations waste enough food to feed them all"
• "this crisis will only deepen"
• Any other valid generalisation from Source 2

Do not accept:
• Specific statistics (these are facts, not generalisations)
• Future predictions unless they generalise about groups
• Statements from other sources
```

## Question 1(b)(ii) [2 marks]

### Format
Point-based marking. Award up to 2 marks for explanation of why the example is that concept.

### Template
```
1(b)(ii) [2 marks]
Award up to 2 marks for explanation of why the example is a [concept].

Indicative content:
• Definition element 1 [1]
• Definition element 2 [1]
• Application to the specific example [1]

Award 2 marks for a full explanation that:
- Defines what a [concept] is
- Explains why the chosen example fits this definition

Award 1 mark for:
- Partial definition OR
- Identification of [concept] feature without full explanation
```

### Example (Generalisation)
```
1(b)(ii) [2 marks]
Award up to 2 marks for explanation of why the example is a generalisation.

Indicative content:
• A generalisation makes a broad statement about a whole group [1]
• It does not acknowledge exceptions or individual differences [1]
• Words like "every," "millions," "all" indicate universal claims [1]
• The statement assumes something is true for everyone in a group [1]

Award 2 marks for explanation that shows understanding of what makes
a generalisation AND applies this to the chosen example.

Award 1 mark for partial understanding, e.g., "It talks about everyone"
without explaining why this makes it a generalisation.
```

## Question 1(c) [6 marks] - Table E

### Level Descriptors

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **3** | 5-6 | Describes the perspective in detail with good coverage of relevant elements. Response is well-developed with clear links between ideas. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Describes the perspective with some relevant elements. Response shows understanding but may lack detail or development. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited description of the perspective. Response may be superficial, list-like, or only partially relevant. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Indicative Content Template
```
1(c) [6 marks]
Describe [organisation]'s perspective on [topic].

Indicative content (ICEVA framework):

Issue:
• [What the organisation sees as the main problem]

Causes:
• [Cause 1 they identify]
• [Cause 2 they identify]

Effects:
• [Effect/consequence 1]
• [Effect/consequence 2]

Values:
• [What they believe is important]
• [Principles guiding their view]

Actions:
• [Solutions they propose]
• [What they want to happen]

Note: Responses do not need to cover all ICEVA elements to reach Level 3,
but should demonstrate detailed understanding of the perspective.
```

## Question 1(d) [8 marks] - Table F

### Level Descriptors

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **4** | 7-8 | Thorough explanation with well-developed reasoning. Clear justification for why the chosen factor is most significant. May compare with other factors. Uses evidence effectively. |
| **3** | 5-6 | Good explanation with developed reasoning. Justification is clear but may lack full development or comparison. Uses some evidence. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Some explanation with basic reasoning. May identify a factor as significant but justification is limited or underdeveloped. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited response. May identify a factor but with little or no explanation of significance. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Indicative Content Template
```
1(d) [8 marks]
Which [cause/effect/factor] is most significant? Explain why.

Indicative content:

Possible factors to discuss:
• [Factor 1 from sources]
• [Factor 2 from sources]
• [Factor 3 from sources]

For each factor, responses might consider:
• Scale of impact
• Number of people affected
• Long-term vs short-term effects
• Root cause vs symptom
• Ability to address/solve

Strong responses will:
• Clearly identify one factor as most significant
• Explain WHY it is more significant than others
• Use evidence from sources to support reasoning
• May acknowledge other factors while justifying choice
• Reach a clear conclusion
```

## Question 2(a) [8 marks] - Table G

### Level Descriptors

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **4** | 7-8 | Thorough evaluation of both strengths AND weaknesses. Well-developed points with clear reasoning. Shows sophisticated understanding of research methodology. |
| **3** | 5-6 | Good evaluation of strengths and weaknesses. Points are developed but may lack balance or full sophistication. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Some evaluation present. May focus more on one aspect (strengths OR weaknesses) or points may be underdeveloped. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited evaluation. May only identify strengths OR weaknesses with little explanation. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Indicative Content Template
```
2(a) [8 marks]
Explain the strengths and weaknesses of the research.

Indicative content:

Strengths might include:
• [Specific strength from the research methodology]
• [Another strength]
• [Good sample size/method/questions]

Weaknesses might include:
• [Specific weakness from the research methodology]
• [Another weakness]
• [Limitations in generalisability/sample/method]

Evaluation criteria to consider:
• Sample size - Is it large enough?
• Sample selection - Is it representative?
• Method - Is it appropriate for the research question?
• Questions - Are they leading/neutral, open/closed?
• Reliability - Could it be repeated?
• Validity - Does it measure what it claims to?
• Generalisability - Can findings apply beyond this sample?

Note: Responses must address BOTH strengths AND weaknesses to access
Level 3 and above.
```

## Question 2(b) [8 marks] - Table H

### Level Descriptors

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **4** | 7-8 | Thorough explanation of how to test the claim. Clear, detailed methodology with appropriate methods and evidence. Shows sophisticated understanding of research design. |
| **3** | 5-6 | Good explanation with clear methodology. Methods are appropriate but may lack full detail or sophistication. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Some explanation of testing approach. Methods may be vague, limited, or not fully appropriate for the claim. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited response. May mention research but without clear methodology for testing the specific claim. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Indicative Content Template
```
2(b) [8 marks]
Explain how the claim '[claim from Source 3]' could be tested.

Indicative content:

Research design elements:
• Research question derived from the claim
• Target population/sample
• Sample selection method
• Sample size justification
• Research method(s) - survey, interview, observation, experiment
• Specific questions or procedures
• How data would be analysed
• How findings would test the claim

Strong responses will:
• Design research that directly tests the claim
• Choose appropriate methods for the claim
• Include specific details (sample size, questions, etc.)
• Explain how findings would confirm or refute the claim
• Consider practical aspects of the research
```

## Question 3 [16 marks] - Tables A + B

**Split marking: Analysis (8 marks) + Evaluation (8 marks)**

### Table A: Analysis [8 marks]

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **4** | 7-8 | Thorough analysis of BOTH arguments. Identifies and explains reasoning, evidence, and language use in detail. Clear understanding of both positions. |
| **3** | 5-6 | Good analysis of both arguments. Identifies key elements but may lack full detail or balance between speakers. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Some analysis present. May focus more on one argument or analysis may be superficial. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited analysis. May describe rather than analyse, or only address one speaker. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Table B: Evaluation [8 marks]

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **4** | 7-8 | Thorough evaluation with clear, justified judgement. Compares arguments effectively. Uses speakers' words to support evaluation. Reaches well-reasoned conclusion. |
| **3** | 5-6 | Good evaluation with judgement. Comparison is present but may lack full development. Conclusion is clear. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Some evaluation present. Judgement may be asserted rather than justified. Limited comparison. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited evaluation. May state preference without justification or fail to compare. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Indicative Content Template
```
Question 3 [16 marks]
Which argument is more convincing, [Speaker A]'s or [Speaker B]'s?

Indicative content:

[Speaker A]'s argument:
• Main position: [summary]
• Reasoning: [logical arguments used]
• Evidence: [facts, statistics, examples cited]
• Language: [emotive/persuasive techniques]
• Strengths: [what's convincing]
• Weaknesses: [what's less convincing]

[Speaker B]'s argument:
• Main position: [summary]
• Reasoning: [logical arguments used]
• Evidence: [facts, statistics, examples cited]
• Language: [emotive/persuasive techniques]
• Strengths: [what's convincing]
• Weaknesses: [what's less convincing]

Comparison points:
• Quality of evidence
• Strength of reasoning
• Use of emotive language
• Acknowledgement of counter-arguments
• Overall persuasiveness

Note: There is no "correct" answer. Credit responses that justify their
judgement with reference to the speakers' words and ideas.
```

## Question 4 [20 marks] - Tables C + D

**Split marking: Reasoning (10 marks) + Evaluation (10 marks)**

### Table C: Reasoning [10 marks]

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **5** | 9-10 | Excellent reasoning with well-developed arguments. Clear recommendation with thorough justification. Effective use of sources and/or own ideas. |
| **4** | 7-8 | Good reasoning with developed arguments. Clear recommendation with good justification. Good use of evidence. |
| **3** | 5-6 | Sound reasoning with some development. Recommendation is clear but justification may lack full development. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Basic reasoning present. Recommendation may be stated but justification is limited. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited reasoning. May state preference without clear justification. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Table D: Evaluation [10 marks]

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|-------|-------|------------|
| **5** | 9-10 | Excellent evaluation considering multiple perspectives. Thorough consideration of different arguments. Balanced assessment of alternatives. |
| **4** | 7-8 | Good evaluation with consideration of different perspectives. May lack full balance or development. |
| **3** | 5-6 | Sound evaluation with some consideration of alternatives. May focus mainly on chosen recommendation. |
| **2** | 3-4 | Basic evaluation. Limited consideration of different perspectives or alternatives. |
| **1** | 1-2 | Limited evaluation. Little or no consideration of alternatives or different perspectives. |
| **0** | 0 | No creditworthy response. |

### Indicative Content Template
```
Question 4 [20 marks]
Which action would you recommend, and why?

Actions being considered:
• Action A: [description]
• Action B: [description]
• Action C: [description]

Indicative content:

For each action, responses might consider:
• How it addresses the issue
• Evidence from sources supporting it
• Practical advantages
• Potential limitations
• Who benefits/who might oppose
• Short-term vs long-term impact
• Cost and feasibility

Strong responses will:
• State clear recommendation
• Provide well-reasoned justification
• Use evidence from sources effectively
• Consider different perspectives
• Acknowledge strengths of alternative actions
• Reach justified conclusion

Note: There is no "correct" action. Credit responses that justify their
recommendation with clear reasoning and consideration of alternatives.
```

---

## General Marking Principles

1. **Best fit:** Apply the level that best describes the quality of the response
2. **Positive marking:** Reward what candidates demonstrate, not what they omit
3. **Indicative content:** Examples are illustrative, not exhaustive
4. **Own ideas:** Credit valid ideas not in indicative content if relevant
5. **Source use:** Credit effective use of source material with or without direct quotation
6. **Balance:** For higher levels, responses must address all required elements (e.g., BOTH strengths AND weaknesses)
