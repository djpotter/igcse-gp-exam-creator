### Source 1: Factual Data
- **Word count:** 15-80 words
- **Reading level:** F-K Grade 4.4-10.5 (higher complexity acceptable)
- Statistics from authoritative sources (UN, WHO, government)
- May include tables, infographics, definitions
- Neutral, informational tone
- Used for Q1(a) - identifying facts/figures

### Source 2: Organisational Perspective
- **Word count:** 57-131 words
- **Reading level:** F-K Grade 5.2-9.8, Flesch RE 47-71
- Viewpoint of charity, NGO, or action group
- Contains claims, opinions, values, bias
- Discusses causes, effects, proposed actions
- **CRITICAL:** Must contain 2-3 clearly quotable examples of the target critical thinking concept for Q1(b)
- Used for Q1(b), Q1(c), Q1(d)

**Quotability Requirements for Critical Thinking Concepts:**

| Concept | Required Language Markers |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Generalisation | "always," "never," "all," "every," "most people," "millions" |
| Prediction | "will," "going to," "in the future," "eventually" |
| Claim | Statements presented as fact requiring evidence |
| Opinion | "we believe," "in our view," "I think" |
| Value | "important," "should," "must," "right," "wrong" |

### Source 3: Student Research Project
- **Word count:** 113-177 words
- **Reading level:** F-K Grade 5.1-9.1 (**EASIER** - authentic student voice)
- Describes fictional student's research
- Includes methodology (surveys, interviews, sample details)
- Contains findings with percentages/statistics
- Has built-in strengths AND weaknesses for evaluation
- **CRITICAL:** Must contain a specific testable claim for Q2(b)
- Used for Q2(a) and Q2(b)

**Authenticity Markers for Source 3:**
- First-person narrative ("I decided to...", "My research found...")
- Simple sentence structures
- Clear methodology description accessible to students

### Source 4: Debate Between Two Speakers
- **Word count:** 232-269 words (TOTAL for both speakers)
- **Reading level:** F-K Grade 6.2-9.5 (varied)
- Two named individuals with opposing views
- Each presents arguments with evidence
- Both use reasoning, evidence, and emotive language
- Used for Q3

**Balance Requirements for Source 4:**
- Roughly equal word counts per speaker (within 20%)
- Equal number of evidence points per speaker
- Similar complexity of reasoning
- Culturally diverse names
- No stereotypical speaker-position associations

## Step 1a: Readability Assessment (NEW in v3.0)

**Complete this step AFTER creating the Source Booklet but BEFORE creating the Question Paper.**

See **[source-readability-criteria.md](references/source-readability-criteria.md)** for full assessment guidance.

### Quick Assessment Process

1. **Count words** in each source and total insert
2. **Calculate average sentence length** (words ÷ sentences)
3. **Estimate Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level**
4. **Compare against targets** in the table above
5. **Flag sources outside range** for revision

### Assessment Decision Tree

```
For each source:
│
├─► Word count within range?
│   ├─ NO → Revise (expand or condense)
│   └─ YES → Continue
│
├─► Average sentence length 10-14 words?
│   ├─ NO → Revise (break up or combine sentences)
│   └─ YES → Continue
│
├─► F-K Grade Level within range?
│   ├─ TOO HIGH → Simplify vocabulary, shorten sentences
│   ├─ TOO LOW → Add appropriate complexity
│   └─ WITHIN RANGE → Continue
│
└─► CONTENT VERIFICATION (Critical)
    ├─► Source 1: Stats/facts for Q1(a) preserved?
    ├─► Source 2: 2-3 quotable concept examples preserved?
    ├─► Source 3: Methodology + testable claim preserved?
    └─► Source 4: Two distinct opposing positions preserved?
```

### If Revision is Needed

When rewriting a source for readability:

**Too Complex (High F-K Grade, Low Flesch RE):**
- Break long sentences into shorter ones
- Replace multi-syllable words with simpler synonyms
- Remove unnecessary jargon
- Add context for technical terms

**Too Simple (Low F-K Grade, High Flesch RE):**
- Combine short choppy sentences
- Add appropriate topic-specific vocabulary
- Include more detailed explanations

**After ANY revision:** Re-verify that all required exam content is preserved.

## Question Paper Structure

See **[specimen-question-templates.md](references/specimen-question-templates.md)** for exact question formats.

### Question 1 [18 marks] - Based on Sources 1 and 2

| Part | Focus | Marks |
|------|-------|-------|
| (a) | Identify fact/figure from Source 1 | 1 |
| (b)(i) | Identify ONE EXAMPLE of [specific concept] from Source 2 | 1 |
| (b)(ii) | Explain WHY the example is that concept | 2 |
| (c) | Describe perspective (NO scaffolding in question) | 6 |
| (d) | Justify most significant cause/factor | 8 |

#### Q1(b) - Critical Thinking Concept (IMPORTANT)

**The question PRE-SPECIFIES the concept.** Students find an example of it—they do NOT identify what type of statement something is.

✅ **CORRECT FORMAT:**
> (b)(i) Identify one example of a generalisation from Source 2. [1]
> (b)(ii) Explain why the example you identified is a generalisation. [2]

❌ **INCORRECT FORMAT:**
> (b)(i) What type of statement is this? Tick one box: Claim / Opinion / Prediction / Generalisation

**Critical Thinking Concepts (choose ONE per exam):**
- **Generalisation** - Broad statement about a group based on limited evidence
- **Prediction** - Statement about what will/might happen in the future
- **Claim** - Statement presented as fact needing evidence to verify
- **Opinion** - Personal belief/judgement that cannot be proven
- **Value** - Belief about what is important/right
- **Bias** - One-sided view that doesn't consider all perspectives
- **Vested Interest** - Personal stake that may affect objectivity

#### Q1(c) - Describe Perspective [6 marks]

**NO SCAFFOLDING in the question.** ICEVA is a marking framework, not student scaffolding.

✅ **CORRECT FORMAT:**
> (c) From Source 2, describe [organisation name]'s perspective on [topic]. [6]

❌ **INCORRECT FORMAT:**
> (c) Describe the perspective. You may consider: the issue, causes, effects, values, actions...

**ICEVA Framework (for marking only):**
- **I**ssue - The main problem
- **C**auses - Reasons why it exists
- **E**ffects - Consequences/impacts
- **V**alues - Beliefs/principles guiding the perspective
- **A**ctions - Proposed solutions

#### Q1(d) - Most Significant [8 marks]

**Keep simple. Two sentences maximum.**

✅ **CORRECT FORMAT:**
> (d) Sources 1 and 2 suggest causes of [topic]. Which cause do you think is the most significant? Explain why. [8]

❌ **INCORRECT FORMAT:**
> (d) Which cause do you think is most significant? Justify your answer using evidence from the sources and your own ideas. You should compare with other causes and reach a conclusion.

### Question 2 [16 marks] - Based on Source 3

| Part | Focus | Marks |
|------|-------|-------|
| (a) | Evaluate strengths AND weaknesses of research | 8 |
| (b) | Explain how to test a claim from the research | 8 |

#### Q2(a) - Research Evaluation [8 marks]

✅ **CORRECT FORMAT:**
> (a) Explain the strengths and weaknesses of the research outlined in Source 3. [8]

#### Q2(b) - Testing a Claim [8 marks]

✅ **CORRECT FORMAT:**
> (b) '[Direct quote of claim from Source 3].'
> Explain how this claim could be tested. You should consider the research methods and evidence that could be used. [8]

**Evaluation Points for Q2(a):**
- Sample size and selection method
- Research methods used
- Question design (open/closed, leading/neutral)
- Response rate
- Geographic/demographic limitations
- Researcher influence
- Generalisability of findings

**Testing a Claim for Q2(b):**
- Research question
- Sample selection
- Method (survey, interview, observation)
- Specific questions/procedure
- How analysis tests the claim

### Question 3 [16 marks] - Based on Source 4

**EXACT FORMAT (do not modify):**

> 3 Study Source 4.
>
> Which argument is more convincing, [Speaker A]'s or [Speaker B]'s?
>
> Your answer should:
> • consider both arguments
> • evaluate their reasoning, evidence and use of language
> • support your judgement with their words and ideas. [16]

**DO NOT add additional bullet points.** Keep exactly three bullets as shown.

Split marking: Analysis (8) + Evaluation (8)

---

### Question 4 [20 marks] - Using All Sources

**EXACT FORMAT (do not modify):**

> 4 [Context sentence: A government/organisation wants to achieve X.]
>
> The following actions are being considered:
> • [Action A]
> • [Action B]
> • [Action C]
>
> Which one of these actions would you recommend to [the government/organisation], and why?
>
> In your answer, you should:
> • state your recommendation
> • give reasons and evidence to support your choice
> • use the material in the sources and/or any of your own ideas
> • consider different arguments and perspectives. [20]

**DO NOT add bullets about** "explaining how it addresses the issue" or "discussing advantages and limitations."

Split marking: Reasoning (10) + Evaluation (10)

## Student Feedback Feature

See **[student-feedback.md](references/student-feedback.md)** for comprehensive feedback guidance.

When reviewing student work, assess:
1. **Use of sources** - Are they citing specific evidence?
2. **ICEVA coverage** - For Q1(c), which elements are addressed?
3. **Evaluation depth** - Are strengths AND weaknesses discussed?
4. **Comparative reasoning** - For Q1(d), Q3, Q4: Are alternatives compared?
5. **Justified conclusions** - Is there a clear, reasoned ending?

**Key examiner insights on common errors:**
- Confusing claim with fact (claims need verification)
- Confusing opinion with value (values are about what's important)
- Only describing, not evaluating (must assess strengths/weaknesses)
- Missing the "most significant" comparative element
- Not reaching conclusions for higher-mark questions
- Not evaluating BOTH speakers in Q3
